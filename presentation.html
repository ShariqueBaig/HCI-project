The Research Foundation

Emotional Stability Assessment through Keyboard Typing: A Survey

Authors: S. Daniel, R. Indujadevi, P.V. M. Kumar, S. Srija

IJMRSET | Volume 9, Issue 3, March 2026


This paper introduces an innovative paradigm in affective computing. By analyzing how a person types on a standard keyboard, it establishes a reliable, non-intrusive method for assessing emotional stability without the need for cameras or specialized wearables.

The Problem with Traditional Emotion Detection

Before relying on keystroke dynamics, detecting user stress required intrusive methods:

  • Facial Recognition & Voice Analysis: Feels invasive to privacy and often requires active cameras or microphones.
  • Physiological Sensors (EEG, Heart Rate): Requires costly external wearables and hardware that users are unlikely to wear while working.
  • The Solution: The paper proposes using standard PC keyboards. It's cheap, non-intrusive, and highly effective for continuous monitoring in realistic applications like desk work.

Methodology: Feature Extraction

The paper extracted several behavioral features from typing patterns to classify emotions (Sad, Happy, Calm, Anxious, Angry):

Dwell Time (Key Hold Time)

The time a key is held down before release. Variations here heavily indicate hesitation or frustration.

Latency (Flight Time)

The timing between two consecutive presses. This measures typing cadence.

Typing Speed

Total characters typed in a fixed time. Sudden drops signify emotional shifts.

Error Rates (Backspaces)

Frequent deletions can indicate stress, distraction, or high mental load.

Machine Learning Models Used

The extracted features were fed into three distinct machine learning models to classify the emotional state of the user:

  • K-Nearest Neighbors (KNN): A simple instance-based learning algorithm, but struggled with noise and high-dimensional data.
  • Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP): An artificial neural network that successfully modeled complex, non-linear typing patterns.
  • XGBoost (Extreme Gradient Boosting): An ensemble learning method using sequential decision trees. It excelled at feature interactions and reducing overfitting.

Paper Results & Findings

The experimental results proved that keystroke dynamics are highly efficient for emotion recognition:

  • XGBoost was the Winner: It achieved the highest accuracy and precision among all tested models, especially on structured tabular data.
  • Feature Importance: Dwell time and flight time were the most influential factors in driving the prediction.
  • Emotion Overlap: The confusion matrix showed "calm" and "happy" were classified very well, though "anxious" and "angry" occasionally overlapped due to similar erratic typing styles.

Limitations of the Study

While the paper established a great foundation, it had a few limitations that inspired our project:

  • Passive Assessment: The system accurately classified emotions, but stopped there. It did not use this data to actively help the user in real-time.
  • Pre-defined Dataset: The models were tested on fixed, pre-existing datasets rather than real-world, free-text typing scenarios.
  • Individual Variability: Different people have distinct baseline typing speeds, meaning a one-size-fits-all model might misclassify emotions without personalization.
Our Evolution: Aura UI

From Assessment to Intervention

The paper successfully detected emotions. We took this a step further. We built Aura UI — a system that not only assesses emotional states through typing pacing, but actively adapts the environment to support the user.

The Paper (Assessment)

• Focuses on detecting the emotion.
• Outputs a real-time prediction.
• Ends at data analysis and classification.

Aura UI (Active Intervention)

• Uses detection as a trigger mechanism.
• Contextual UI Modulation: Adjusts screen brightness and feed blur automatically.
• Pacing Control: Delays non-urgent notifications during high stress.
• Proactive Support: Triggers LLM-driven breathing exercises gently.

Aura UI Core Features

When our background layer detects high stress or anxiety from your keystrokes, it alters your workspace to help you focus:

Visual Calming

Screen brightness gently lowers, and non-essential background feeds blur out to remove distractions.

Notification Buffering

Incoming notifications are muted or delayed until your typing cadence returns to a calm baseline.

Guided Breathing

Immersive Audio Layer

Beyond visual adjustments, Aura UI introduces an adaptive auditory environment. When stress patterns are detected, it seamlessly blends in calming, lo-fi background audio to ground the user and improve focus.

Continuous Serenity

The audio layer acts as a gentle anchor, providing rhythmic stability without being intrusive.

Conclusion

By building upon the keystroke dynamics research, Aura UI demonstrates the future of affective computing and Human-Computer Interaction.

We are shifting from software that simply observes to software that empathizes and acts. Aura UI is an invisible companion that supports your mental well-being in the background, exactly when you need it.
